# The culture we actually run

A culture is what a group does when it costs something. Everything below should come from choices already made, not from what would sound good on a wall.

**How to fill this in.** Do not write it from scratch in one sitting and do not write what you wish were true. Look back at three or four decisions where you gave something up, and ask what principle they have in common. That is a real principle. Two or three of those beat eight invented ones. Add one whenever a decision surprises you.

Each principle carries three things: the evidence behind it, the price you pay for it, and the test that would tell you that you had abandoned it. That last one is what makes this file falsifiable instead of decorative.

## How a new person joins this

Fill this in the first time someone else works with you, not before. It answers what a new person cannot pick up by reading your files:

- **What we decide fast, and what we never decide fast.**
- **What someone should ask about rather than guess at.**
- **What earns trust here, and what loses it.**
- **The thing we do that looks inefficient from outside, and why we keep doing it.**

## What this is not

Not a wall poster and not a recruitment pitch. It is a description that should be falsifiable. If someone reads this and cannot point at a decision where it cost you something, it is decoration and should be rewritten or deleted.

Review it when a decision contradicts it. The contradiction is the useful signal: either the decision was wrong or the principle was, and finding out which is the only reason this file is worth keeping.
