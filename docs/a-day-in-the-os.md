# A day in the OS

[docs/what-this-can-do.md](what-this-can-do.md) lists every capability installed on your machine. It is generated from the skills on disk, so it cannot lie about what is there. What it cannot show you is what any of it feels like in a real week.

This page does that. Nine situations, each one a person with a problem, what they say, and what actually comes back.

**Read the outcomes carefully.** There are no percentages on this page and no multipliers. Nobody here saves eleven hours a week. Every outcome below is something the OS mechanically does, stated at the size it actually is, and you can check each one against the skill named beside it. A page like this is exactly where invented numbers go, which is why there are none.

---

### 1. Monday morning, and you have lost the thread

**Rana runs a six-person design studio.** Last week ended badly and she cannot remember what she promised anyone.

She says: **"What's on for today?"**

The OS reads her own files and returns one screen: her anchor task for today, decisions still open, flags past their review date, the last three things she logged, and her next calendar event if a calendar is connected.

**What she actually gets:** the state of her business as she last wrote it down, in one screen, without opening six files. Not a plan. Not advice. Her own week, handed back to her.
*Skill: `today`*

---

### 2. You just got off a call and the details are already leaving

**Tomás closed a scoping call with a prospect** and has nine minutes before the next one.

He says: **"I just got off a call with Priya at Meridian. She wants the audit but not until Q4 budget opens. She mentioned their ops lead Sam is the actual blocker."**

The OS files it: the account updates, the timing goes to the pipeline, and Sam is added to the names glossary as an unconfirmed person, because Tomás said the name once and spelling a stranger's name wrong into a permanent record is how a system starts lying to you.

**What he actually gets:** the call is in the system in one sentence, and Sam stays flagged as unconfirmed until Tomás says otherwise.
*Skills: `brain-log`, `capture-sweep`, the unconfirmed-facts ledger*

---

### 3. The thing you were supposed to do three weeks ago

**Wei has been telling herself she will follow up with a client "this week"** for a month.

She does not ask. The OS raises it at the weekly review: the commitment has rolled two weeks with no movement, and it wants a decision. Keep it, kill it, or escalate it with a real deliverable and a date.

**What she actually gets:** the thing she was avoiding gets named out loud, once a week, by something that is not her. She still decides. The OS never kills anything on its own.
*Skill: `weekly-review`, stall detection*

---

### 4. Nine things on the list, no idea which one

**Ade is stuck** and has been rereading the same list for twenty minutes.

He says: **"Cut this list down. I can't think straight."**

The OS reads his priorities, his open commitments, and what is actually blocked, then names one thing and says why the others wait.

**What he actually gets:** one item, with the reasoning shown so he can disagree with it. This is the piece a solo founder has no one to do: not more analysis, someone to close the loop.
*Skill: `priority-triage`*

---

### 5. Writing something that has to sound like you

**Sofia needs a client update** and every draft she writes reads like a press release.

She says: **"Draft the monthly update for Halden."**

The OS reads her voice profile (built once, from an interview it ran with her), reads what actually happened on that account from her own log, and drafts in her register with the real detail in it.

**What she actually gets:** a draft that sounds like her and references things that actually happened, because both came out of her own files. She still edits it. Every founder does.
*Skills: `client-update`, the voice profile, `anti-ai-writing`*

---

### 6. A number in your deck that you cannot actually back

**Kwame is building a market case** and the research came together fast, which is the problem.

He says: **"Check the claims in this."**

The OS reads the finished document back and reports what is uncovered: numbers with no source, a quote attributed to someone with no link, "no other tool does this" (nobody can verify that, and it is the line that gets challenged first), and one row in his cost table where the arithmetic does not reconcile.

**What he actually gets:** the four or five claims that would have been questioned in the room, found while he can still fix them. It warns and never blocks, because it cannot tell which numbers are load-bearing. He can.
*Skill: `claims_check.py`, `rules/research-integrity.md`*

---

### 7. Someone is about to score your business

**Fen has an investor meeting in eleven days** and has never been through diligence.

She says: **"What would an investor ask me about this?"**

The OS scores five dimensions against evidence in her own files, and where the files do not answer, it says so rather than filling in the gap. Then it lists the questions a diligence analyst would ask, most of which she cannot yet answer.

**What she actually gets:** the hard questions eleven days early instead of live in the room, plus an explicit list of what her own records do not say about her own business.
*Skill: `self-diligence`*

---

### 8. You broke something and it is 11pm

**Nadia asked for a cleanup** and the OS rewrote a file she needed.

She says: **"Undo that."**

Every file the OS edits is snapshotted before the write. She gets it back. Before restoring, it saves her current state too, so the undo itself cannot lose anything.

**What she actually gets:** the file back, no git command typed, no support ticket. The net covers the last twelve sessions of edits made through the OS's own editing tools.
*Skills: `changes`, session snapshots, `caveman_git.py`*

---

### 9. The month you realise it is compounding

**Marc has been logging for six weeks.** He asks about a client he has not touched since April.

The answer comes back with the decision he made in April, why he made it, what the client said on the call, and the two things he flagged as risks at the time. He had forgotten all three.

**What he actually gets:** the reason the habit is worth keeping. Nothing here is retrieval magic; it is plain-file search over things he wrote down. The system did not know anything he did not tell it. It just did not forget.
*Skills: `brain-pass`, `query`*

---

## The honest boundary

The OS runs when you open a session, when a hook fires, and when you ask. It does not watch your inbox all day, it does not act while you sleep, and it will not send anything without your yes. That is the trade for having no server, no account, and no copy of your business on someone else's machine.

And the compounding in scenario 9 is not free: it needs six weeks of scenario 2. If you write nothing down, this is a folder of empty files with good intentions in the front matter.
