# v1.47.0 - it remembers, and correctly

v1.46 taught the OS to tell the truth about the numbers it produces. This release makes it tell the truth about the past it holds. A three-lens audit ran over the whole build - the new-user path, the architecture, and data retrieval - with every key claim verified by running the shipped scripts, not by reading the docs. The finding in one line: the OS filed things well and read them back badly. Everything here is one of those two directions, fixed inside the machinery that already existed.

## What changed

- **The memory skills read is now current.** Twenty-five skills read `brain/.snapshot.md` as "your state right now", and nothing refreshed it - from day 2 they presented week-old flags as today's. Every reader now checks the snapshot's date and regenerates past 3 days, and the session-close hook regenerates it automatically, so in normal use it is simply always fresh.
- **What the OS has learned about you finally reaches its output.** `brain/patterns.md` records what keeps happening (3 or more repetitions, with evidence). No output skill ever saw it. The snapshot now carries your active patterns, so a draft or a plan can say "this keeps happening" instead of treating every week as the first.
- **Your archive is searchable.** After about seven weeks of use, the log ager moves history into `brain/archive/` - which search excluded. Keyword search, timeline, and even exact-ID lookup said "not found" about entries on your own disk. All three modes now see the archive.
- **Your mobile captures are searchable.** Everything captured away from the laptop lands in `brain/rants/`, which search skipped unless you knew to say a magic word. Raw captures now always rank - below distilled entries, never invisible.
- **A misspelled name is no longer a silent nothing.** The names glossary that fixes spellings when you capture now also works when you ask: "what did Janice say" finds the note filed under "Jansi", and a near-miss name gets a "Did you mean" instead of a blank.
- **"What happened in March" has an answer now.** Timeline reads the dates on your entries instead of dating whole files, and takes `--from` / `--to` for a direct range. Search results cite the entry that matched, not whatever sat at the top of the file.
- **Fourteen day-one paper cuts.** The first-session card no longer promises a prompt that refuses; the ZIP instructions warn about Windows' folder-inside-a-folder; `/next` and `/update` stop refusing to run on normal installs; setup says the honest 90 minutes instead of 40; day 2 opens with an offer instead of a jargon scold; day 1's `/today` asks you for one thing instead of showing `[NOT SET]`; the seeded examples say they are examples; and the session brief drops its permanent line of shell jargon.
- **The health check sees the sharpest silent failure.** The skills that fire when you describe a situation ("log this", "what's on my plate") stop firing natively when their copies drift - and `/verify` used to say PASS through it. It now catches the drift and names the one-command fix.

## Why it matters to you

**If you have been logging for months:** ask about something from March. Before this release the honest-looking answer was "not found". Now it is your own entry, with its date and ID.

**If you capture on your phone:** those notes were in the one folder search skipped. They are findable now without knowing any magic word.

**If you use the writing skills:** drafts stop being aimed at last week. The state they read is refreshed at every session close, and it now includes what keeps happening, not just what is open.

## Integration protocol

1. **Take the system-layer files.** Everything is engine, docs, and hooks. Nothing writes into your data folders.
2. **Run once:** `python scripts/skills_sync.py --apply` then `--check`, so your natively-discoverable skills match their sources.
3. **Prove the fix on your own history:** `python scripts/query.py --mode timeline --from <some past month>-01 --to <that month>-28`. If you have archived months, entries you have not seen in a long time come back.
4. **Nothing to migrate.** No file format changed. Your log, flags, and patterns files work as they are - the readers got better, not stricter.

## No Claude Code? The by-hand path

Two of these fixes are habits you can hold in any tool. When you archive old notes, keep the archive inside whatever your search covers - an archive outside the search scope is a polite way of deleting things. And when a search over your own notes returns nothing, check the spelling of every name in the question against how you actually wrote it down before you trust the nothing - a one-letter miss reads exactly like an empty history.
