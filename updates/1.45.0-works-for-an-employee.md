# v1.45.0 - works for an employee, not only a founder

The setup wizard has asked since the role fork landed whether you own the business or run a role inside one. Until now the second answer led into a quietly degraded install: no snapshot for the propose engine to read, a next-move seat that could never run, and copy that kept calling you the founder. This pack is that answer finally leading somewhere.

## What changed

An employee running part of a job is the operator of that part, and the OS now serves that scope end to end:

- **The Role Snapshot.** `core/identity.md` gains the operator twin of the Founder Snapshot: the part of the job you run, who you answer to, what is yours to own, what is not yours to decide, and the single biggest blocker. The wizard captures it for the operator role with the same manners as the founder flow - one question at a time, everything skip-able, never an invented answer. Each install carries exactly one of the two blocks.
- **The propose engine has an operator path.** "What should I do next" reads the Role Snapshot and names the single highest-leverage move on the work you own, in front of the person waiting on it - one big step, two small enough to start today. The SessionStart brief words its nudge the same way.
- **The account manager serves both readings.** For a founder it drafts the client update. For an employee it drafts the status update to whoever you answer to - say "write my status update". Same formats, same draft-only gate; sending stays yours.
- **The copy no longer assumes you own the company.** README, the first-day doc, the ramp, the tour, and the shipped templates now speak of the operator - the person the work lands on. A founder reading loses nothing.

## Why it matters to you

**If you run a role inside a company:** this is the release that makes the OS yours. Set up fresh, answer "operator" at the role question, and the whole machine - snapshot, propose engine, morning loop, status updates - works on your part of the job.

**If you are a founder:** nothing changes in what the OS does for you. The copy got wider, not different. You can skip this pack entirely and lose nothing.

**If you set up as an operator before v1.45:** your identity file has no snapshot block, so the propose engine still falls back to the generic ranking. The integration protocol below closes that in five lines.

## Integration protocol

1. **Take the system-layer files.** Skills, commands, and scripts update automatically; nothing above writes into your data folders.
2. **If you are a founder, stop here.** Your Founder Snapshot and everything that reads it are unchanged.
3. **If you run a role inside a company and set up before v1.45:** open `core/identity.md` and add the block. Copy the `## Role Snapshot` section from `templates/identity.md`, fill the five lines in your own words, and delete the `## Founder Snapshot` section if the wizard ever wrote you an empty one - each install carries exactly one of the two. Or just say: "I run a role inside a company - capture my Role Snapshot" and answer the questions.
4. **Check the Role line.** Under `## Basics` it should read `**Role:** operator`. Downstream skills gate on that token.
5. **Ask "what should I do next".** With the block filled, the propose engine runs your path and closes with one big step and two small ones. That is the whole feature working.

## No Claude Code? The by-hand path

**Copy one block: the `## Role Snapshot` section from `templates/identity.md` into your `core/identity.md`, and fill its five lines.**

That is most of the value with no tooling at all: the five lines - scope, who you answer to, yours to own, not yours to decide, blocker - are the sharpest one-page description of a job you can write, and every reasoning pass over your file gets better the moment they exist.
