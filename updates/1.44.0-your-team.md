# v1.44.0 - meet your team

Your OS now introduces itself as five named roles instead of a folder of files. This is the headline change of the release and the only one that changes what a first run feels like.

## What changed

`roles/employees.yaml` used to ship empty, with a note saying to add your first row once a job had recurred three times. That was honest and it was also the reason almost nobody ever opened the file. Nothing arrives, so nothing gets used, so nothing recurs.

It now ships with five roles:

| Role | What it gives you | Say |
|---|---|---|
| The assistant | Your morning: at most four questions drawn from what is actually waiting, each answer written into the file that owns it | "run my morning" |
| The one who names your next move | The single step toward your next paying customer, or toward the work you own if you run a role inside a company | "what should I do next" |
| The note-taker | Whatever you captured away from your desk, filed into your brain with its date and source kept | "catch up" |
| The account manager | The update or progress report for a client, or for whoever you answer to, in your voice, drafted and left for you to send | "write the update for [client]" |
| The reviewer | A review of the other four, proposing changes to how they are defined | "review the assistant" |

Every one of them is backed by a skill that is on your disk, every one carries a written job description and an exact list of what it may touch, and every one arrives `gated`, which means defined with nothing run yet.

Three things arrived with them that had been missing:

- **The layer now actually installs.** `roles/employees.yaml`, `rules/digital-employees.md` and the two scripts behind them (`employee_verdict.py`, `flat_yaml.py`) were in the repo since v1.43 but were never on the setup wizard's copy list, so no install had ever received them. The `employee-review` skill would have failed on its first command. Four other scripts were in the same state and are fixed in the scripts pack.
- **The doctrine says what the file does.** `rules/digital-employees.md` argued that an empty org chart is honest. It now argues the sharper version: a chart that lies is what is dishonest, a gated row backed by a skill you can open is an introduction, and only a run you saw may move a row to `active`.
- **Propose-only and draft-only are separated.** A propose-only job writes nothing and holds no write tool. A draft-only job writes a file and stops before anything leaves your machine. They were being called the same thing, and the charter audit treats them differently, so the doctrine now names both.

## Why it matters to you

**If you are installing for the first time:** this is most of what you will remember about setup. You will be handed five roles and told what each one gives you tomorrow morning, rather than shown a file tree.

**If you have been running the OS for months:** the five roles describe jobs your OS was already doing. What is new is that they are named, scoped, and gradable, so "the follow-up thing keeps surfacing stuff I already handled" becomes a change to one row rather than a correction you make again next week.

**You can skip this pack** if you do not want an org chart. Set `modules.employees: no` in `os-config.yaml` and nothing here is offered to you again. Deleting the five rows is also fine; nothing else reads them.

## Integration protocol

1. **Take `roles/employees.yaml` if you do not have one.** Almost certainly you do not: it was never on the copy list. Accept it whole, comments included. The header is what explains the file to you in six months.
2. **If you already wrote your own rows**, the update proposes the five starter rows alongside yours rather than replacing the file. Read that diff and decline any role you do not want. Your rows are never touched.
3. **Take the two scripts.** Without `employee_verdict.py` and `flat_yaml.py` the org chart is a file nothing reads and `employee-review` errors on its first command.
4. **Audit the charters once**, so you can see what the grants actually are rather than trusting the prose:
   ```
   python scripts/employee_verdict.py charters
   ```
   It names any row whose tool grant is wider than its job description claims. On the five shipped rows it reports clean.
5. **Run one role this week.** Say "run my morning". Then record what you saw:
   ```
   python scripts/employee_verdict.py verdict --employee daily-assistant --verdict ok --why "asked about the thing I was avoiding"
   ```
   That verdict is the whole input to every review later. Skipping it is free and costs you the review.
6. **Move a row to `active` only after a run you watched.** Never as tidying.

## No Claude Code? The by-hand path

**Copy one file: `templates/roles/employees.yaml` to `roles/employees.yaml`.**

Then read it. It is five job descriptions in plain language, and reading them is most of the value even with no tooling at all: it tells you what jobs are worth handing to a system, what each is allowed to touch, and where the line sits between drafting something and sending it.

If you want a second thing, open `rules/digital-employees.md` and read the section called "The charter IS the grant". It is one page and it is the rule that stops "never sends" from being a wish.
