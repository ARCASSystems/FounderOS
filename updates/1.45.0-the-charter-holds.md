# v1.45.0 - the charter holds, and the boundary lists agree

A third-pass architecture review of this release found the product's sharpest claim standing on prose. The role registry said "the charter IS the grant - there is no second list to drift from," while a second list existed in every seat skill's own frontmatter, wider on all five seats. This pack is that seam closed, plus the ownership boundary lists brought into agreement so an uninstall can never delete what an update protects. (Correction, v1.45.1: the first print of this pack called the skill list "the one Claude Code actually enforces." Claude Code today reads it as pre-approval, not a wall - the boundary is a written contract plus the drift audit, and anything outside a grant routes through your normal permission prompts.)

## What changed

- **Every seat's declared tool list now matches its charter.** The five starter skills (morning-loop, founder-next-move, catch-up, client-update, employee-review, plus `today` in the assistant's chain) had blanket `Bash` or unused tools in their `allowed-tools`. Each is narrowed to the exact scoped grants its charter row states - `Bash(python scripts/employee_verdict.py:*)` instead of a whole shell.
- **The charter audit now checks the seam.** `python scripts/employee_verdict.py charters` reads each seat skill's `allowed-tools` against its registry row and names any blanket grant or drift between the two. A wider enforced list can no longer hide behind a clean audit.
- **The doctrine says what the boundary really is.** `rules/digital-employees.md`, the registry header, and the first-day doc now state the true mechanism: the grant lives in two places kept identical - the row you read, the frontmatter the skill declares - with the audit as the tripwire and your own permission prompts as the backstop.
- **Uninstall and update agree on what is yours.** Default uninstall now preserves `capture/`, `brands/`, `roles/`, `system/`, `rules/`, and `os-config.yaml` - paths it would previously have deleted while the update command protected them. `roles/` and `system/` join the User Layer lists in both the update command and the install doc.
- **The review loop has real triggers.** The morning loop refreshes and reads the org-chart view (so REVIEW DUE actually surfaces daily), and the weekly retro gains a grade-the-team step. The capture filer's charter now names the glossary writes and file moves it always performed.
- **Stale caches refresh instead of rotting.** The propose engine and housekeeping regenerate `brain/.snapshot.md` when it is older than 3 days, not only when it is missing, and housekeeping's auto-fix now actually writes the file.

## Why it matters to you

**If you run any of the five roles:** the boundary you were promised ("never sends" as a permission, not a wish) is now the boundary that holds at run time, and the audit tells you if a future edit widens it.

**If you customised a seat or added your own:** run `python scripts/employee_verdict.py charters` once. Any drift between your registry rows and your skill grants is named with the file and the fix.

**If you ever uninstall:** your employee registry, your quarantine record, your rules, and your brand config now survive the default uninstall, matching what updates always promised.

## Integration protocol

1. **Take the system-layer files.** All of the above are engine files; nothing writes into your data folders.
2. **Your `roles/employees.yaml` is yours and is not replaced.** If you installed before this pack, copy three row details from `templates/roles/employees.yaml` into your registry by hand if you want them: the capture filer's `tools` and `may_write` lines, and the assistant's `inputs` and `may_write` lines.
3. **Run the audit once:** `python scripts/employee_verdict.py charters`. Clean means your rows and your skills agree. A finding names the exact file and field to align.

## No Claude Code? The by-hand path

**Open each seat skill you use and check its `allowed-tools` line against the matching row in `roles/employees.yaml`. Make the two lists identical, narrowest wins.**

That is the whole mechanism with no tooling: the row is the contract you read, the skill list is what your AI tool enforces, and any time the two differ the wider one is the bug.
