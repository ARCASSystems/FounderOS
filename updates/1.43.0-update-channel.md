# v1.43.0 - the update channel pack

How updates reach you from here on. This is the pack that makes the other three safe to accept, and the one that matters most if you have personalized your install.

## What changed

**`/founder-os:update` now walks update packs**, not just files. It pulls the new System Layer as before, then walks the packs newer than your version and integrates each one with you. Same command, richer behavior. There is no second update command to learn.

**`os-config.yaml`** at your OS root, written by the setup wizard. Two jobs:

- **Which modules you actually run.** An update pack declares which modules it touches, and the parts about modules you never adopted are skipped rather than walked through and dismissed.
- **Your brand values.** Your document font and the author name on generated documents. Shipped code reads them from here and never assumes a default. Where a value is unset, the pre-ship gate says the check was skipped rather than passing on a guess.

**Three-way merge on anything you may have edited.** Three versions exist: the old shipped file, the new shipped file, and yours. The update works out what the release actually changed and proposes that change on top of your version, leaving your edits alone. You see a diff and say yes or no, per file. Never a batch yes, and if a merge cannot be done cleanly you get told plainly and your own version is what stays on disk.

**Every pack ends with a by-hand path** naming the one file to copy to get most of the value with no tooling. A pack that cannot state that does not ship. If a change cannot be described as a by-hand step, it is too complicated to be an update.

## Why it matters to you

**If you have edited your `CLAUDE.md` or anything in `rules/`:** this is the pack that protects that work, and it is the reason to take it before the others. The failure it prevents is quiet and expensive. An overwrite takes your edits away and you find out weeks later, when something you fixed is broken again and you cannot remember fixing it.

**If you skip modules:** filling in `os-config.yaml` is worth two minutes. It is the difference between an update that talks to you about your install and one that talks to you about the product.

**If you are on a ZIP install with no git:** the by-hand path in every pack is written for you specifically. You are not a second-class install, and any release that cannot describe itself as a by-hand step is a release that will not ship in this form.

**Nobody can skip this pack** if they intend to take future updates. It is the mechanism the rest arrive through.

## Integration protocol

1. **Read the diff on `.claude/commands/update.md` carefully.** This is the command doing the updating, so a bad merge here is the one that hurts. It gains one step (walk the packs) and a section on the three-way merge. If you have customized your update command, take the diff and re-read the whole file afterwards.
2. **Fill in `os-config.yaml` by hand, now.** The wizard writes it on a fresh install, but your install already exists, so nobody has filled it in for you. Two minutes:
   - `brand.document_author` - your own name, as it should appear as the author on a document you send. Without it, generated Office files credit the library that made them.
   - `brand.font` - only if you have one. Leave it unset otherwise, and the font check stays honestly skipped.
   - `modules` - `yes` or `no` for each of the ten. Be accurate rather than aspirational: `no` on something you have not adopted is what makes future updates shorter.
   - `install.method` - `zip`, `git`, or `plugin`.
3. **Confirm the merge behavior once, on something safe.** Next time an update proposes a change to a file you have edited, check that your edits are still there afterwards. If they are not, stop and report it. That is the whole promise of this pack and it is worth verifying once rather than trusting.
4. **Skim `updates/README.md`.** One page. It tells you what to expect from every future pack, so you can read the next one in thirty seconds instead of five minutes.
5. **Then take the other three packs in any order.** Doctrine, scripts, daily loop. They are independent, and each one names who can skip it.

## No Claude Code? The by-hand path

**Copy one file: `os-config.yaml`.**

Drop it at your OS root and fill in the four things listed above. That single file is what makes every future update able to skip what does not apply to you, and it is what stops shipped code from assuming your brand.

The other half needs no file at all, only a habit: **when a new release changes a file you have edited, never overwrite it.** Open both versions side by side, find what the release actually changed, and apply just that on top of yours. Tedious once, and it is the difference between an OS that accumulates your judgment and one that quietly resets it.

Every pack from here will name the one file to copy, so you can stay on this path indefinitely without falling behind on anything that matters.
