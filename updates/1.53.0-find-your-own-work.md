# v1.53.0 - find your own work

A founder went through four separate chats looking for a project she had been working on for days, and finished the search convinced it was nowhere to be found - that the assistant had done the work and saved it somewhere she could not see. Then, after being told exactly where it was, she said she could not understand any of the answer.

Nothing was lost. Every file was exactly where it had been written. But the folder sat inside her OS with git told to ignore it, so it appeared in no status line, no backup, and no answer she could act on - and when she finally asked, she got a wall of paths, layer names, and remotes back. Two failures, one release.

## What changed

- **`/founder-os:where` is new - ask where your work is, in the words you would actually use.** "where is my work", "where did that go", "where did you save it", "I cannot find the vendor list". It answers with the project and the folder, in your language, most recent first. Not a table. Not an architecture explanation.

- **The folders most likely to be lost are now the ones most clearly named.** A project folder that git ignores is invisible to every safety net the OS has, which means the work at the highest risk of vanishing was the work nothing ever mentioned. That is backwards. `where` reports every folder it finds and says plainly, per folder: this exists only on this computer, or this is backed up. It volunteers that finding even when you did not ask for it, because a founder who does not know cannot decide to fix it.

- **It is honest about a ZIP install.** No version history means no second copy of anything, and the output says exactly that rather than staying quiet, which reads as reassurance.

- **The register is the feature.** The skill carries the failed answer and the working answer side by side as examples, and bans the words that made the first one unreadable: System Layer, User Layer, untracked, gitignored, the remote. All true, none of them English. It also never reorganizes what it found - a founder asking where something is has not asked you to move it, and moving it is what makes the next search harder.

- **Read-only, standard library, no key.** `python scripts/where.py` opens nothing, moves nothing, renames nothing. It runs when someone already thinks they have lost something, which is the worst possible moment to be clever.

## Why it matters to you

**If you have ever lost track of your own work:** this is the fastest way to stop trusting a system, and it has nothing to do with whether the system worked. Ask, get a sentence back, carry on.

**If you run more than one project inside your OS:** you almost certainly have folders with no backup right now. Run `/founder-os:where` once and find out. That single line is worth more than the search feature.

**If you are the technical one and someone else runs the OS:** the failed answer in this release note was correct, complete, and useless. Worth reading as a specimen.

## Integration protocol

1. **Copy `templates/scripts/where.py` into your `scripts/`.** No personalization in it; a straight copy is correct.
2. **Run it once, today:** `python scripts/where.py --all`. Read the WORTH KNOWING block at the bottom before anything else.
3. **If it names folders with no backup**, that is the real finding. `own-your-history` fixes it on one yes and takes a minute.
4. That is all. Nothing else in your install changes and no behaviour anywhere else is affected.

## No Claude Code? The by-hand path

**Once a week, open your project folder and sort by date modified.** That is the whole search feature, and it works in Explorer, Finder, and Drive. Then ask the question this release is really about: *if this laptop died tonight, which of these folders would be gone?* Anything living in exactly one place goes into a synced folder or a zip on a drive today. The discipline is worth more than any tool: the folders you never think about are the ones with no copy, precisely because you never think about them.
