# v1.44.0 - the first 30 days

Your OS now ramps up on a written schedule instead of arriving at full speed on day one.

## What changed

A new file, `cadence/first-30-days.md`, with real dates in it, and the morning loop reads it before it does anything else.

- **Days 1 to 7.** The OS watches. It files what you tell it and asks one question a day. No queue items raised on its own, no unasked drafts, no volunteered opinions about your priorities.
- **Days 8 to 21.** It starts raising things unasked: queue items for what went quiet, the morning loop at its full four questions. Nothing acts without a yes.
- **Day 22 onward.** It acts inside the gates you already approved.

One boundary the stages never move: what you ask for, you get, at every stage. The ramp limits what the OS starts on its own, never what you request - a day-2 "what should I do next" gets a real answer, and if your files are too thin to answer well, saying so is the answer.

Three details that are deliberate:

- **There is no scheduler.** The dates are written in the file and the morning loop compares today against them. Nothing runs on a timer, nothing fires while you are away.
- **A missing file means full speed.** An older install and someone who deleted the ramp are the same case, and neither should find their OS has gone quiet for a reason they cannot see.
- **The stage 3 line about nothing leaving your machine is not part of the ramp.** That is the floor. It reads the same on day 400 as on day 1.

## Why it matters to you

**If you are installing now:** the first week will be quieter than you expect, and that is the point. Anything the OS proposed in week one would be built on the handful of answers you gave the wizard in an afternoon. A week of your actual work is the cheapest way to make the first real proposal land instead of embarrass itself. Setup tells you this at the end so the quiet is not mistaken for something broken.

**If you have been running for months:** this changes nothing for you. You have no ramp file, the morning loop reads that as stage 3, and it behaves exactly as it did. Take the file only if you want a written record of what the OS is allowed to do, which some people want and most do not.

**If you want it at full speed today:** say "skip the ramp", or write `Ended [today's date] - going straight to stage 3` at the top of the file yourself. The file was built to be overruled.

## Integration protocol

1. **Decide whether you want it at all.** An install that has been running for months has already earned its trust the slow way. Declining this is a normal answer and nothing else in the release depends on it.
2. **If you take it**, write today's date as `Started:` and set the three stage dates from it: day 7, day 8, day 21, day 22. Write real dates, not relative ones. A date you can read is the whole design.
3. **Read the three stages once.** If any of them is more or less than you want, change it now rather than discovering the mismatch on day 9.
4. **Then leave it alone.** The morning loop reads the file. There is nothing to run and nothing to keep current.

## No Claude Code? The by-hand path

**Copy one file: `templates/cadence/first-30-days.md` into your `cadence/` folder, and fill in the five dates by hand.**

Even with no tooling, having decided in writing what you will let a system do in week one, week three, and month two is worth the ten minutes. Most people never decide it and end up settling it argument by argument, which is how the boundary quietly moves.
