# v1.43.0 - the daily loop pack

One new command and two behavior changes to surfaces you already read every day. Files only. Nothing to install, nothing to keep running.

## What changed

**`/founder-os:morning-loop`** asks at most four questions drawn from what is genuinely waiting: an item blocked on a decision, an ask nobody has answered, a commitment gone quiet, a name still unconfirmed, a flag past its review date. Each question comes with two or three narrow options and a recommendation first. Then every answer is written into the file that owns it, and the thing that raised it gets closed in the same pass.

It ends with one coach line: yesterday's step scored from the files where the files can tell, and today's single step named.

**`/today` and `/next` changed what they consider progress.**

- Surfaces report what **moved** - a reply that came in, a decision that landed, a payment that arrived - rather than how many items were closed. When nothing moved, that is the line.
- Work needing your own hands surfaces as an action in your words ("call the supplier back about the delivery date"), not as another entry on a list. A full plate is never answered by adding to it.

**Two templates**, both empty on purpose. `templates/brain/needs-attention.md` is where the OS parks a question only you can answer, and answered rows stay in place so a returning question is distinguishable from a new one. `templates/workflow-map.md` maps each role to the workflows underneath it, splitting stages into the deterministic ones that belong in code and the judgment ones needing a model plus a check.

## Why it matters to you

**If you have ever stopped trusting a daily brief:** this is the pack for you, and both changes here are about why that happens. A brief that always looks productive is one you stop reading by the second week, because you know a busy day and a day that moved something are not the same thing and the brief does not. Counting outcomes instead of closures makes a genuinely quiet day report itself as quiet. That is the only version worth opening.

**If the OS keeps asking you things you already decided:** the second half of the morning loop is the fix. Every answer closes its source in the same pass. An answered question that leaves its source open comes back tomorrow wearing the same clothes, and by the third morning you have learned to ignore the loop.

**If your mornings are already fine:** skip the command and take the two behavior changes anyway. They cost nothing and they make `/today` honest.

**One thing this pack deliberately does not do:** it does not add a dashboard. There is nothing to open, nothing running in the background, and nothing to check. Four questions, in the same place you already work.

## Integration protocol

1. **Take the morning-loop skill and command as they are.** New files, nothing of yours at risk.
2. **`/today` and `/next` need real diffs read.** Both are files founders commonly edit. `today.md` gains one section on what counts as progress, `next.md` gains one step on what it is allowed to recommend. Read each separately and decline either if you have tuned that command to something you prefer. They are behavior rules, not machinery: declining them leaves everything working.
3. **Copy `templates/brain/needs-attention.md` into `brain/`** if you want the loop to have somewhere to read your open asks from. Without it the loop still runs and draws from the queue, cadence, and flags instead. It just has one less place to look.
4. **Run it once tomorrow morning**, before the day starts making decisions for you:
   ```
   /founder-os:morning-loop
   ```
   Watch for one thing specifically: whether every answer visibly lands somewhere. The report names the file per answer. If an answer went nowhere, that is a bug worth reporting, and it is the failure this whole design exists to prevent.
5. **Expect a quiet first run.** On a fresh install almost nothing qualifies, so it will say "Nothing needs you this morning" and stop. That is correct behavior and not a broken install. It gets useful once you have a queue with items in it and a week of log history behind you.
6. **Leave `templates/workflow-map.md` empty** until a workflow actually recurs. A map of workflows you intend to build stops matching reality within a month.

## No Claude Code? The by-hand path

**Copy one file: `skills/morning-loop/SKILL.md`.**

The whole loop is instructions, not code. Paste that file into any capable assistant along with your queue, your commitments, and your open asks, and say "run this". It will ask the four questions and tell you where each answer belongs. You do the writing.

If you want the idea without any file at all, it reduces to two habits worth having on their own. First: each morning, answer at most four questions about what is actually waiting, and write each answer into the file that owns it rather than leaving it in a chat. Second: whenever you answer something, close the thing that asked, or it will ask again tomorrow.

The second habit is the one that matters, and it is the one almost every system gets wrong.
