# v1.50.0 - the team grows through one door, and every seat becomes someone you can talk to

v1.49 gave the team a memory of its own runs. This release closes the half you actually feel: how a new team member comes to exist, right-sized instead of oversized, and how you manage one without learning a second system.

## What changed

- **`hire` is new - the Chief of Staff's door.** Say "I need help with X" or "this keeps eating my week". The OS walks a six-rung shape ladder - an answer, a preference line, a skill, a script, a chartered seat, a team - and proposes the SMALLEST shape that removes the job, with one sentence on what the next rung up would have bought. Most recurring pains die at rung one to four and never needed an agent at all. Being told that plainly is the point: the one hire that IS proposed arrives with your trust intact.

- **Every hire carries an informed-choice passport before your yes.** Five lines, plain words: what it may touch, what it never does, what it costs to run, what leaves the machine (nothing, unless a connector is named), and how to fire it. If you are new to agents, these five lines are the difference between consenting and hoping. The same rule covers anything from outside the OS - a plugin skill pack, an MCP connector, a tool: proposed with its exact source and its own passport, one yes each, **never installed silently**.

- **A team is a workflow whose handoffs you can read.** When a team is genuinely earned (several different specialist judgments in sequence - and `hire` refuses the label when one seat would do), each judgment stage gets one seat, deterministic stages stay in code, and each stage's read-list names the previous stage's output. `python scripts/agent_runs.py list` then reads back as a relay: who did what, in what order, where the baton was dropped. You manage the team the way you manage one seat - there is no second surface to learn.

- **`scripts/agents_sync.py` is new - one org chart, two faces, zero drift.** Every non-retired row in `roles/employees.yaml` generates one `.claude/agents/<seat>.md`: the job, the measure, the read-list, the charter's tools verbatim, the prohibitions, and the run-record closing act. Talk to a seat by name, dispatch it as a subagent, change its standing instructions by editing its row and re-running apply. A hand-written agent file is named and never touched. Firing a seat is one line: set the row to `retired`, run apply, the agent file goes with it.

- **The doctrine stopped disagreeing with itself.** v1.49's run-log section had landed in the template copy of `rules/digital-employees.md` but not the root copy an in-place install reads. Both copies now match and both carry the new shape-ladder section.

## Why it matters to you

**If a job keeps eating your week:** say so, in those words. What comes back is the smallest fix and the price of the bigger one - not a sales pitch for an agent army, and not another one-off chat that fixes nothing.

**If you were about to build a "team of agents" because everyone is:** the ladder will probably talk you down to something cheaper, and it will say why. When it does propose a team, the handoffs are traceable and every member has a written contract. That is the version of a team that survives month two.

**If you would rather nothing new ever gets installed:** nothing is. Every capability from outside the OS arrives as a proposal with provenance and a passport, one yes each. Silence never installs anything.

## Integration protocol

1. **Take the system-layer files.** Nothing here writes into your data folders on update.
2. **Copy `scripts/agents_sync.py`** (from `templates/scripts/`) into your `scripts/`.
3. **Generate your agent files once:** `python scripts/agents_sync.py apply`. Each seat on your org chart becomes an addressable agent. If you have hand-written files in `.claude/agents/`, they are named and left alone.
4. **If you edited `rules/digital-employees.md`,** take the two new sections by hand: "Why the runner records the run" (v1.49, if your copy missed it) and "How a job gets created (the shape ladder)".
5. **Prove the loop in one command:** retire any seat you never use (set its row to `retired`), run apply, and watch its agent file disappear. That is the whole management model - the row is the contract.

## No Claude Code? The by-hand path

**Before you build any assistant for a recurring job, write one sentence naming the job as an outcome, then ask: would an answer, a written rule, a documented procedure, or a small script remove it?** Only when all four fail does the job deserve an agent - and when it does, write the agent's contract before the agent: what it may touch, what it must never do, and how you will know a run happened. The ladder is the feature. The files in this release are just plumbing that walks it for you.
